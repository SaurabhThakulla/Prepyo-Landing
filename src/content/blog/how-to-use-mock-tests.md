Many students take mock test after mock test and wonder why their score stays the same. A mock test is a measuring tool. It tells you where you are; it doesn't move you forward on its own. What raises your score is what you do *after* the mock.

## Why mocks matter

A full mock test under real conditions shows you things short practice can't:

- **Stamina:** whether your concentration lasts the full two to three hours.
- **Timing:** whether you finish each section within its limit.
- **Your real weak spots:** which skills and question types lose you the most marks.
- **Test-day nerves:** practising under pressure makes the real thing feel familiar.

## Take mocks like the real test

A mock only tells the truth if you take it seriously:

- **Do it in one sitting**, at the same time of day as your real test if possible.
- **No pausing**, no checking answers, no phone.
- **Use headphones** for listening and speak aloud for speaking, as you will on test day.

## The review: where the score actually improves

Plan to spend **as long reviewing a mock as taking it**. Here's a simple method.

### Step 1: Sort every lost mark

Go through each wrong or weak answer and give it a reason:

| Reason | Example |
|---|---|
| Didn't know | A word, grammar rule or fact I hadn't learned |
| Careless | Spelling, a plural, a word limit, a misread instruction |
| Timing | Ran out of time or rushed |
| Strategy | Used the wrong approach for the question type |
| Delivery | Hesitations, restarts or unclear pronunciation (speaking) |

### Step 2: Count the reasons

After sorting, count each reason. Most students find one or two reasons cause most of their lost marks. That's your priority list.

### Step 3: Make a fix list

Turn each reason into one specific action:

- *Careless spelling* → 10 Write from Dictation sentences daily (PTE), or a spelling list for listening (IELTS).
- *Timing in reading* → time every passage this week.
- *Didn't know vocabulary* → 15 collocations a day.
- *Speaking hesitations* → daily record-and-replay practice.

### Step 4: Redo the questions you got wrong

A few days later, redo every question you got wrong without looking at the answer. If you still get one wrong, it stays on your list.

## Read the trend, not one score

Scores move up and down between mocks, because different tests have different topics and difficulty. Look at the trend across three or four mocks, skill by skill. Steady improvement in your weakest skill is the best sign that your preparation is working.

## How often to take mocks

- **Week 1:** one diagnostic mock to find your starting point.
- **Middle weeks:** one mock a week, with the days in between spent on your fix list.
- **Final week:** two mocks, then light review and rest before the test.

## When you're ready to book

A good rule: book your test once **two mocks in a row** reach your target score. One good score could be luck; two in a row is a pattern.
