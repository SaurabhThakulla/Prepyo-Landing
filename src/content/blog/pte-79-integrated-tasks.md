"PTE 79+" means scoring at least **79 in each of the four communicative skills**: listening, reading, speaking and writing. One weak skill is enough to miss the target, even if your overall score is high.

The fastest way to lift all four is to understand **integrated tasks**. In PTE Academic, many tasks count towards more than one skill. Get one of them right and two scores go up. Get it wrong and two go down.

## The integrated tasks

| Task | Counts towards |
|---|---|
| Read Aloud | Reading and speaking |
| Repeat Sentence | Listening and speaking |
| Re-tell Lecture | Listening and speaking |
| Summarize Written Text | Reading and writing |
| Reading & Writing: Fill in the Blanks | Reading and writing |
| Summarize Spoken Text | Listening and writing |
| Write from Dictation | Listening and writing |
| Highlight Incorrect Words | Listening and reading |

Since August 2025, PTE also includes **Summarize Group Discussion**, where you listen to several speakers and summarise the discussion aloud, and **Respond to a Situation**, a spoken reply to an everyday scenario.

## Why this matters for 79+

Because these tasks feed two skills, they are where practice pays off twice. Three of them deserve extra attention:

**Read Aloud.** It's short, appears early and is scored on both content and how you say it. Clear pronunciation and a steady pace, with no long pauses or restarts, matter more than a perfect accent.

**Repeat Sentence.** You hear a sentence once and repeat it. Every word you reproduce counts, so practise holding phrases in memory rather than single words, and keep speaking even if you miss part of it.

**Write from Dictation.** You hear one sentence and type it exactly. Spelling and every word count. It's short, but a strong source of both listening and writing marks, so practise until you rarely miss a word.

## Templates won't save you anymore

For years, students memorised fixed templates for Describe Image, Re-tell Lecture and the essay. Since August 2025, Pearson's scoring combines AI with human review, and **memorised templates are detected and penalised**.

Learning a *structure* is still useful: an opening, two or three key points, a conclusion. Fill it with what you actually see or hear, in your own words.

## A weekly routine that works

1. **Daily:** 15 minutes of Read Aloud and Repeat Sentence, recorded so you can listen back.
2. **Daily:** 10 Write from Dictation sentences. Check every spelling mistake.
3. **Three times a week:** one Summarize Written Text and one Summarize Spoken Text, keeping strictly to the word limits.
4. **Weekly:** one full mock test under real timing. Then spend more time on the tasks that lost the most marks than on the ones you already do well.

## Measure, don't guess

The hardest part of PTE speaking is that you can't judge your own fluency and pronunciation. Record yourself and listen back. Better still, use practice that marks every word, so you can see exactly which sounds and pauses cost you marks.
