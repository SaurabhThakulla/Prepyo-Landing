Hangul, the Korean alphabet, was designed to be easy to learn, and it is. Its letters are built logically and each block of letters is one syllable. For EPS-TOPIK, reading Hangul fluently is the foundation for everything else, so it's worth spending one focused week on it.

## How Hangul works

Korean is written in **syllable blocks**. Each block has:

1. A **first consonant**,
2. A **vowel**, and sometimes
3. A **final consonant** underneath (called *batchim*).

For example: **ㅎ + ㅏ + ㄴ = 한** (han), and **ㄱ + ㅡ + ㄹ = 글** (geul). Together, **한글** spells *Hangul*.

If a syllable starts with a vowel sound, it is written with the silent consonant **ㅇ** in front: **아** (a), **이** (i).

## The 24 basic letters

**14 consonants:**

ㄱ (g/k) · ㄴ (n) · ㄷ (d/t) · ㄹ (r/l) · ㅁ (m) · ㅂ (b/p) · ㅅ (s) · ㅇ (silent at the start, *ng* at the end) · ㅈ (j) · ㅊ (ch) · ㅋ (k) · ㅌ (t) · ㅍ (p) · ㅎ (h)

**10 vowels:**

ㅏ (a) · ㅑ (ya) · ㅓ (eo) · ㅕ (yeo) · ㅗ (o) · ㅛ (yo) · ㅜ (u) · ㅠ (yu) · ㅡ (eu) · ㅣ (i)

Notice the pattern: adding a small extra stroke to ㅏ makes ㅑ, adding a *y* sound. The same happens with ㅓ→ㅕ, ㅗ→ㅛ, ㅜ→ㅠ.

## The seven-day plan

**Day 1: the ten basic vowels.** Write each one ten times while saying it aloud. Practise with ㅇ in front: 아, 야, 어, 여, 오, 요, 우, 유, 으, 이.

**Day 2: the first seven consonants** (ㄱ ㄴ ㄷ ㄹ ㅁ ㅂ ㅅ). Combine each with every vowel: 가, 나, 다, 라, 마, 바, 사…

**Day 3: the other seven consonants** (ㅇ ㅈ ㅊ ㅋ ㅌ ㅍ ㅎ). Again, combine each with every vowel. Notice how ㅋ, ㅌ, ㅍ, ㅊ are "breathier" versions of ㄱ, ㄷ, ㅂ, ㅈ.

**Day 4: final consonants (batchim).** Add a consonant underneath: 각, 간, 갈, 감, 갑, 강. Learn that many final consonants sound alike: ㄷ, ㅅ, ㅈ, ㅊ, ㅌ and ㅎ at the end of a syllable all sound like a short *t*.

**Day 5: double consonants and compound vowels.** Double consonants are tense sounds: ㄲ, ㄸ, ㅃ, ㅆ, ㅉ. Compound vowels combine basic ones: ㅐ and ㅔ (both sound like *e*), ㅘ (wa), ㅝ (wo), ㅟ (wi), ㅢ (ui) and others.

**Day 6: read real words.** Read signs and words you'll meet at work: 출구 (exit), 입구 (entrance), 화장실 (toilet), 위험 (danger), 금연 (no smoking). Don't worry about meaning yet; focus on reading the sounds correctly.

**Day 7: review and speed.** Read a short list of 50 words aloud with a timer. Repeat it until you can read the list twice as fast as the first time.

## Rules that confuse beginners

- **Sounds carry over.** When a syllable ends in a consonant and the next starts with ㅇ, the consonant moves across: 한국어 is read *han-gu-geo*, not *han-guk-eo*.
- **ㄹ changes.** At the start of a syllable it's closer to *r*; at the end it's closer to *l*.
- **ㅐ and ㅔ sound almost the same** in modern Korean. Learn which one a word uses by reading it often.

## Stop using romanisation early

Romanised Korean (*annyeonghaseyo*) is useful for the first two or three days. After that, it slows you down. The EPS-TOPIK paper is entirely in Hangul, so train your eyes to read it directly.
