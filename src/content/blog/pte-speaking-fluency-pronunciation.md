In most PTE speaking tasks, part of your score comes from **how** you speak, not just what you say. Two terms come up again and again: oral fluency and pronunciation. Both are widely misunderstood.

## What oral fluency means

In PTE, fluency is about **smooth, even delivery**:

- A steady rhythm, without long pauses.
- No hesitations like *umm* and *ahh*.
- No repeating words or restarting sentences.
- Pausing at natural places, such as commas and full stops, not in the middle of a phrase.

It does **not** mean speaking fast. Racing through a Read Aloud passage usually leads to stumbles, and every stumble breaks your fluency.

## What pronunciation means

Pronunciation is about being **easy to understand**:

- Clear individual sounds, especially the ones that differ from Nepali.
- Correct word stress (*PHO-to-graph*, *pho-TOG-ra-phy*).
- Natural sentence stress, with the important words a little stronger.

It is **not** about sounding British or American. A clear Nepali accent can score well.

## Sounds Nepali speakers often find hard

Every language has its own sound system, and some English sounds don't exist in Nepali. Common ones to practise:

- **V and W:** *very* vs *wary*, *vest* vs *west*.
- **F and P:** *fan* vs *pan*, *coffee*.
- **Z and J:** *zoo*, *easy*, *jazz*.
- **TH:** *think*, *three*, *this*, *that*.
- **Consonant clusters:** *street*, *school*, *spring*. Avoid adding an extra vowel at the start (*is-school*).
- **Word endings:** don't drop the final *s*, *ed* or *t*: *asked*, *months*, *tests*.

Record yourself saying a list of these words, then compare with a dictionary's audio.

## Where fluency and pronunciation count most

They affect your score in Read Aloud, Repeat Sentence, Describe Image, Re-tell Lecture, and the newer tasks Summarize Group Discussion and Respond to a Situation. Read Aloud and Repeat Sentence also count towards reading or listening, so improving delivery there pays off twice.

## Five daily drills (20 minutes)

1. **Shadowing (5 min):** play a short English news clip and speak along with it, half a second behind, copying the rhythm.
2. **Chunking (3 min):** take a paragraph and mark where the natural pauses go, in groups of 3–6 words. Read it aloud pausing only at your marks.
3. **Problem sounds (4 min):** say ten word pairs from the list above, slowly then at normal speed.
4. **Record and replay (5 min):** read one Read Aloud passage, record it, listen back. Note every hesitation and restart.
5. **Keep-going practice (3 min):** describe a picture for 40 seconds without stopping. If you make a mistake, carry on. Never restart.

## On test day

- Start speaking promptly when the microphone opens, as long silences can end the recording.
- Speak at a comfortable volume, about 5 cm from the microphone.
- Ignore the people around you. Everyone is speaking at once in the test room.
- If you lose your place, keep going with the next phrase rather than going back.
