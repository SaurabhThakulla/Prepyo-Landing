IELTS Listening is the section where the most marks are lost to small, avoidable mistakes: a misspelled word, a missed plural, an answer over the word limit. Fix those habits and your band can rise without your English changing at all.

## The format

- **4 parts, 40 questions, about 30 minutes.**
- **Part 1:** a conversation about an everyday situation, such as booking a room.
- **Part 2:** one person talking about an everyday topic, such as a tour or a facility.
- **Part 3:** a conversation in an academic setting, often students and a tutor.
- **Part 4:** a lecture or talk on an academic subject.

The parts get harder as you go. You hear each recording **once**.

On computer-delivered IELTS you type answers as you listen and get two minutes at the end to check. On the paper test you get extra time at the end to transfer your answers to the answer sheet.

## 1. Read ahead every time you get a pause

Before each section there is time to look at the questions. Use all of it. Read the next questions, not the ones you just finished.

## 2. Predict the answer type

Before the audio starts, decide what kind of answer fits each gap: a number, a name, a date, a place, a plural noun. "The meeting is on ____" needs a day or date. Knowing that tells you what to listen for.

## 3. Watch the word limit

"NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER" means exactly that. Three words is wrong, even if the meaning is correct.

## 4. Beware of corrections

Speakers often change their minds: "We'll meet on Tuesday. Oh, actually, Wednesday is better." The test-makers put these in deliberately. The last answer given is usually the right one.

## 5. Spelling and plurals count

A correct answer spelled wrongly scores zero. Learn the spellings of common test words: *accommodation*, *environment*, *government*, *necessary*, days and months. Check whether the answer should be plural: *two tickets*, not *two ticket*.

## 6. Practise spelled-out names and numbers

Part 1 often spells out names and addresses letter by letter, and reads phone numbers aloud. Practise until letters like *E/I*, *G/J*, *A/R* and numbers like *15/50* never trip you up.

## 7. Listen for paraphrases, not the exact words

The question rarely uses the same words as the audio. If the question says "the cost", the speaker might say "it's 40 pounds". Train yourself to listen for meaning.

## 8. Don't get stuck on one question

If you miss an answer, let it go and move straight to the next. Dwelling on one gap makes you miss the next three.

## 9. Practise with real accents

IELTS uses British, Australian, North American and other accents. If you only ever listen to one accent, the others will slow you down. Mix podcasts, news and lectures from different countries into your week.

## A weekly listening routine

- **Every day:** 20 minutes of English audio with no subtitles: news, podcasts or lectures.
- **Three times a week:** one full IELTS listening part under test conditions, checking every wrong answer to find out *why* it was wrong.
- **Weekly:** a full 40-question test, then a review of spelling, plurals and word-limit mistakes.

Keep a list of every mistake by type. After two weeks, the pattern is usually obvious, and the fix is quick.
