Every year, students book the wrong IELTS module and have to sit the test again. The two versions look similar, but they are not interchangeable. Here's how to choose.

## The short answer

- **Going abroad to study at a university or college?** You almost certainly need **IELTS Academic**.
- **Going abroad to work, for training, or for migration?** You probably need **IELTS General Training**.

But the only rule that really matters: **take the module your university, employer or visa route asks for.** Check their website or ask them in writing before you book.

## What is the same

**Listening** and **Speaking** are identical in both modules: the same recordings, the same questions, the same examiners.

## What is different

### Reading

| | Academic | General Training |
|---|---|---|
| Texts | Long passages from books, journals and newspapers | Everyday texts: notices, adverts, workplace documents, plus one longer text |
| Questions | 40 in 60 minutes | 40 in 60 minutes |
| Scoring | Same 0–9 band scale, but a different conversion from raw score | |

Because the General Training texts are easier, you need more correct answers in General Training to get the same band as in Academic.

### Writing

| | Academic | General Training |
|---|---|---|
| Task 1 | Describe a graph, chart, table, map or process (150 words) | Write a letter: formal, semi-formal or informal (150 words) |
| Task 2 | An essay on a general topic (250 words) | An essay on a general topic (250 words) |

Task 2 is similar in both. The big difference is Task 1: describing data versus writing a letter.

## Choosing based on your plans

**Choose Academic if** you're applying for a bachelor's or master's degree, a diploma at a college, or a professional registration that asks for Academic (some nursing and medical bodies do).

**Choose General Training if** you're applying for skilled work, a work-and-holiday programme, or permanent residence where General Training is accepted.

**Not sure?** If you might study *and* migrate later, Academic is often accepted for both. But "often" isn't "always": check each requirement.

## How preparation differs

If you're taking **Academic**, put extra time into:

- Describing graphs and processes with accurate data language (*rose sharply*, *peaked at*, *accounted for*).
- Reading long, dense passages quickly.

If you're taking **General Training**, focus on:

- Letter tone: a complaint to a company sounds different from a note to a friend.
- Scanning short practical texts for specific information.

For both, Writing Task 2 carries the most weight in your writing score, so practise essays every week.

## Don't forget the test type

Separate from the module, some visa routes, particularly for the UK, need **IELTS for UKVI**, which is the same test taken at approved centres. Check whether your route needs it before booking.
